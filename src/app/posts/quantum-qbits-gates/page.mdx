## Bits that are not stuck

This is post two of [Learn Quantum](/projects/quantum/). Last time a **Cbit** could only sit in $|0\rangle$ or $|1\rangle$. Those two kets are the **computational basis** for one bit-sized system — the labeled axes we expand every state in. A **Qbit** uses the same axes, but its state can be any unit-length mix of them with complex coefficients.<Cite n={1} />

$$
|\psi\rangle = \alpha_0|0\rangle + \alpha_1|1\rangle,
\qquad
|\alpha_0|^2 + |\alpha_1|^2 = 1.
$$

That mix is a **superposition**: both basis kets appear in the state at once, each weighted by an amplitude $\alpha$. The amplitudes are not hidden classical coins waiting to be revealed. Measurement is its own post. Today I care about what states look like, which operations are allowed, and how to read a tiny circuit.

## Superposition is a unit vector

Pick amplitudes whose squared lengths sum to 1. Examples:

$$
|0\rangle,\quad
|1\rangle,\quad
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle),\quad
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle-|1\rangle).
$$

The last two put equal weight on $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$, so a later measurement in this basis would give 50/50 either way. They differ by a **relative sign** (a simple case of relative phase): the second amplitude is $+1/\sqrt{2}$ in one state and $-1/\sqrt{2}$ in the other. Probability bars look identical. Gates still feel the sign, because later interference cares about signed (complex) amplitudes, not just $|\alpha|^2$.

<MathAside title="Qbit vs Cbit in one sentence">
<p>A Cbit's state is one of the two computational-basis kets. A Qbit's state is any unit vector in the complex plane those two span. Multi-Qbit states can also be entangled — not a product of single-Qbit states. I park entanglement for later.</p>
</MathAside>

## Unitary means "length-preserving linear"

Allowed reversible Qbit ops are **unitary**: linear maps that send unit vectors to unit vectors, so they never stretch or shrink the state.<Cite n={1} />

<MathAside title="The unitary badge: U†U = 1">
<p>Write $U^\dagger$ for the conjugate-transpose of $U$ (flip rows↔columns, then conjugate each entry). $U$ is unitary when $U^\dagger U = U U^\dagger = 1$ (the identity). That is exactly the algebra of "has an inverse and preserves lengths." Every gate today — $X$, $Z$, $H$ — passes this test, and each is its own inverse: $X^2=Z^2=H^2=1$.</p>
</MathAside>

Every reversible classical permutation of basis states extends to a unitary on Qbits by linearity. So NOT and cNOT still work. Quantum also adds gates with no classical story, starting with Hadamard and $Z$.

## Meet X, Z, and H

$$
\mathbf X =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 1 \\
1 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\mathbf Z =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & -1
\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\mathbf H =
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 \\
1 & -1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Action on the computational basis:

$$
\mathbf X|0\rangle = |1\rangle,\quad
\mathbf X|1\rangle = |0\rangle
$$

$$
\mathbf Z|0\rangle = |0\rangle,\quad
\mathbf Z|1\rangle = -|1\rangle
$$

$$
\mathbf H|0\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle),\quad
\mathbf H|1\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)
$$

Hadamard is the first gate that turns a basis state into a real superposition you can see on a bar chart. $Z$ is quieter: it leaves probabilities alone and only flips the sign of $|1\rangle$.

Pick $|0\rangle$ or $|1\rangle$, apply $H$, and compare the bars to the signed $\alpha$ line. Negative amplitude still yields the same 50% probability weight.

<HadamardBarsDemo />

<Takeaway>
$H$ spreads a basis state into an equal superposition. Probability cares about $|\alpha|^2$; gates care about the signed (complex) $\alpha$ too.
</Takeaway>

## Gate picker

Start from a basis state. Chain $X$, $Z$, $H$, or identity with Apply — each click multiplies onto the current state. Watch the amplitude bars ($|\alpha|^2$) and the signed $\alpha$ line (phase/sign). Reset when the state gets spicy.

<GatePickerDemo />

<Takeaway>
$X$ flips bits. $Z$ flips the sign of $|1\rangle$. $H$ builds (and can unbuild) superpositions. All three are their own inverses: $X^2=Z^2=H^2=1$.
</Takeaway>

## Circuit diagrams

A wire runs left to right through gate boxes. State in on the left, state out on the right. The cultural clash: math writes operators to the left of the ket, so "H then X" on the diagram is the product $\mathbf X\mathbf H$ in equations.<Cite n={1} />

Step the tiny circuit. Read the active column. Match the bars — and the signed $\alpha$ — to each stage.

<Takeaway>
Diagrams read left → right. Operator products read right ← left. Same history, opposite ink order.
</Takeaway>

## Many Qbits, briefly

$n$ Qbits live in a $2^n$-dimensional space. The computational basis is the $2^n$ classical product states $|x\rangle_n$. A general state is any unit superposition of those basis kets. Algorithms we care about are built from 1-Qbit and 2-Qbit gates chained on those wires.<Cite n={1} />

You do not need the full zoo today. Superposition, unitary, $H/X/Z$, and circuit literacy are enough.

<TryIt>
Compute $\mathbf H\mathbf X\mathbf H|0\rangle$ on paper (or with the picker: Apply H, then X, then H). You should find $\mathbf Z|0\rangle = |0\rangle$. That sandwich is $HXH=Z$.
</TryIt>
