## Classical bits, Dirac costume

This is post one of [Learn Quantum](/projects/quantum/). Last time I walked through vectors and bras/kets. Today I put those same symbols on ordinary classical bits — no superposition yet.<Cite n={1} />

Mermin calls the physical two-state classical system a **Cbit**, so "bit" can stay the abstract 0/1. Every state you meet today is one of the classical possibilities. The payoff is notation and reversible gates that later lift to quantum for free.

## A Cbit is a box with 0 or 1

A **ket** is Dirac's labeled box for a vector: write the label between $|$ and $\rangle$. For one Cbit the two legal states are

$$
|0\rangle,\qquad |1\rangle.
$$

Think "labeled box," not "mystery vector." Classically those are the *only* states. Together they are the **computational basis** (Mermin also says classical basis) for one Cbit: the orthonormal pair we expand everything in. The 2D vector space is mostly empty furniture until Qbits arrive.<Cite n={1} />

Several Cbits sit side by side as a **product** (tensor product, if you want the grown-up name):

$$
|1\rangle|0\rangle|1\rangle
\quad\text{or compactly}\quad
|101\rangle.
$$

Same object, two fonts. I use whichever is readable. The rightmost factor is Cbit 0 (the $2^0$ place); moving left raises the power of two.

## Integer labels

When zeros and ones are a binary integer, pack them:

$$
|5\rangle_3 = |101\rangle.
$$

The subscript is the width $w$: how many Cbits you meant. Without it, $|3\rangle$ might be $|11\rangle$, $|011\rangle$, or $|0011\rangle$. Ambiguous on purpose until you pin $w$.<Cite n={1} />

Flip the width control. Watch the same integer put on different padded costumes.

## Only reversible ops will graduate

Quantum computation (aside from measurement) runs on **reversible** operations: every output came from exactly one input, so you can undo the step. Erase-to-zero is irreversible — from $|0\rangle$ alone you cannot tell whether you started at $|0\rangle$ or $|1\rangle$.<Cite n={1} />

So I only care about permutations of classical computational-basis states. Those permutations are exactly the classical ops that extend to Qbits later.

### NOT on one Cbit

The interesting 1-Cbit reversible gate is NOT, written $\mathbf X$:

$$
\mathbf X|0\rangle = |1\rangle,\qquad
\mathbf X|1\rangle = |0\rangle.
$$

Apply twice and you are home: $\mathbf X^2 = 1$.

### cNOT on two Cbits

**XOR** (exclusive OR) is addition mod 2: $y \oplus 0 = y$ and $y \oplus 1 = 1-y$. Same bit twice cancels: $x \oplus x = 0$.

**Controlled-NOT** (cNOT) keeps a control bit and writes XOR onto a target when the control is 1:

$$
C_{10}|x\rangle|y\rangle = |x\rangle|y \oplus x\rangle.
$$

Control on the left in this labeling; target on the right. Swap the roles and you get $C_{01}$. Click through the truth table. Hit apply twice. Reversibility is the whole point.

<Takeaway>

## SWAP from three cNOTs

You can swap two Cbits with three controlled-NOTs:

$$
S_{ij} = C_{ij}\, C_{ji}\, C_{ij}.
$$

I am not making you prove it in the browser. Just know the classical reversible toolkit is richer than "flip a bit," and cNOT is the workhorse.<Cite n={1} />

## Operators as matrices (optional muscle)

If you like components,

$$
\mathbf X =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 1 \\
1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

cNOT has a $4\times 4$ matrix that permutes the four basis states. Same permutation story, heavier ink. Prefer the ket rules until a calculation forces the matrix.

</TryIt>

## Waiting offstage

Hadamard $\mathbf H$ and $\mathbf Z$ show up in Mermin's classical chapter as algebraic toys. On Cbits they barely mean anything — $H$ would turn $|0\rangle$ into a superposition, which is not a Cbit state. On Qbits they are everyday gates. That is [the next post](/posts/quantum-qbits-gates/).

If you can read multi-Cbit kets, label them as integers, and describe NOT/cNOT, you are done for today.
