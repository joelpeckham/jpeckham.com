Quantum computers sound like sci-fi until you notice the real claim is smaller and sharper: for a few carefully chosen problems, the physics of information lets you do something classical bits cannot. This series is my attempt to learn that claim the slow way — re-teaching N. David Mermin’s *Quantum Computer Science* with interactive demos and extra math hand-holding.

I am not assuming you remember linear algebra fondly. We start with vectors and Dirac notation as tools, not as a hazing ritual. Then Cbits, then Qbits, then the famous algorithms and the few-Qbit protocols. Each article is short on purpose. Skip around only if you already know the earlier vocabulary.

The book stays the north star. The words here are mine. Poke the demos until the symbols stop feeling like furniture.
